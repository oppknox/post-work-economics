Post-Work Economics

The Ownership Dividend

Self-driving fleets, humanoid labor, and cheap intelligence are arriving on an industrial timetable. A stipend of $3,000 to $5,000 a month for every American adult costs on the order of $9 to $16 trillion a year. A tax on robot profits covers a slice. The rest has to be owned.

01 — Premise

The labor arrives on a schedule.

This near future is a stack of industrial facts. Taken together, they make a wage a weaker way to organize income. Communities still need care, craft, and company. Work becomes semi-optional: available, respected, and no longer the only way to eat.

General-purpose robots are discussed at an Elon-scale ramp: on the order of 1 million a year now, 10 million a year within five years, and a path toward a billion machines. The curve below is that ramp drawn as a smooth exponential. It is a planning path, not a forecast.

Method, in one paragraph

Worker-equivalents equal robots divided by two. Value added equals those equivalents times $17.4 trillion over 170 million workers. Profit is scaled so the 200 million robot case is exactly $6 trillion, which is $30,000 a robot, and that rate is applied to every other fleet size. The stipend is the monthly amount times 12 times 260 million adults. A fund’s size is the citizen dividend divided by the yield. Ten to twenty percent of reference profit is $0.6 to $1.2 trillion, or roughly 6 to 13 percent of the $3,000 stipend. The conclusion in the brief stands: taxation of this stream cannot fund it.

03

03 — The ownership model

Citizens hold the claim. Firms run the machines.

Every citizen receives a share of robot and AI productive capacity through a sovereign wealth fund that accumulates stakes as the machines deploy. The fund is a shareholder. It is not a factory and it is not a regulator. As fleets grow, the public claim grows with them. The contribution is a stake in new capital, assigned by law at deployment.

Norway is the existence proof. The Government Pension Fund Global, on the order of $2 trillion, shows that a democracy can hold a vast diversified portfolio and pay the return without operating the companies. Scale that idea about thirty times and you are holding a $60 trillion fund. At a 5% dividend it pays $3 trillion a year.

Read that dividend against the stipend. Three trillion dollars across 260 million adults is about $960 a month. That is a serious income. It is the yield of the $60 trillion sketch, and it is not yet $3,000. A 5% fund that pays the full $3,000 needs about $187 trillion. The $5,000 stipend needs about $312 trillion. The $60 trillion fund is the right kind of object at the first industrial scale. It is the instrument. It is not the last dollar of a $3,000 check.

What closes the rest is a larger claim, a larger fleet, and a lower price level. Owning the entire reference profit stream — all $6 trillion — pays about $1,923 a month. The $3,000 nominal stipend, funded only from that profit, arrives when the US fleet passes 312 million robots and the citizen claim is the whole claim.

04

04 — The tax shift

Tax what remains scarce.

Income tax leans on a base this future thins out. If work is optional, a tax on wages is a tax on a shrinking habit. Keep a thin residual if the politics demand one. Move the weight onto bases that survive automation.

Robot ownership

The claim itself. Equity in autonomous capital, including a stabilizer on the citizen fund’s returns if a future Congress needs one.

Capital gains

The new wage bill accrues to owners. In this model that bill is $30,000 a robot a year. Tax the gain where the profit actually lands.

Land value

Land cannot be automated away. A humanoid does not mint parcels. Taxing land value takes a scarcity rent without punishing the decision to deploy a machine.

05

05 — Transition

Insure the decade, not the century.

The dangerous period is the ramp. A ten-million-robot US fleet is a few percent of the labor force. A two-hundred-million-robot fleet is a different country. Between them, wages in automatable work can fall before the dividend has fully vested.

  1. Now

    Write the claim

    Assign the stake in law while the fleet is still small. The rule should be boring and automatic: capacity comes online, the citizen share is issued.

  2. Years 1–5

    Turn a dividend on early

    Annual production reaches about 10 million robots. The installed US fleet is still modest. Households should already see a deposit, even a small one, so the path is visible.

  3. The ramp

    Wage insurance, and retraining that is not a toll

    Replace part of the loss for workers leaving automatable jobs. Sunset the insurance when the published dividend hits a floor. Offer specific retraining. The course is a tool. The stake is citizenship, and the course is not the price of admission.

  4. Year ~13 on this path

    A second labor force

    About a billion machines worldwide, 200 million in the US, roughly 0.6× human labor. By then the fund is either vested or the profit has a different owner.

06

06 — The anti-pattern

The state should not own the robots.

There is a version of this future in which a ministry buys the humanoids, rosters them, and rents them out. It answers the ownership question by creating a worse one.

Anti-pattern

Regulator owning the regulated

Every private builder is a petitioner. Antitrust has nothing coherent to say, because the monopoly is the government. The next generation of the machine becomes a budget line.

The structure

A shareholder, not an operator

Companies build, deploy, and fail in public. The fund accumulates a prescribed stake as capacity comes online — equity, a royalty on deployed units, or a claim on the profit of autonomous capital. Citizens can see the share. Managers run the robots.

08

08 — The decision

Build the claim before the fleet arrives.

The robots are a capital program. The dividend is the decision to assign part of that capital to citizens as the machines deploy, rather than discovering afterward that the profit has an owner and the public has a tax.

Three to five thousand dollars a month is the design target. A profits tax is a companion. Ownership is the base. Wage insurance covers the decade in which the two labor forces change places. The ministry does not get the keys.
